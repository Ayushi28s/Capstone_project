# Employee Roles & Approval Workflow

## The three roles
- **Manager** — full access to every tool in CommerceOps AI, including the Approval Queue.
  A Manager's own requests are never gated for approval.
- **Tier 1 Support** — front-line order status, refund, and billing-dispute handling. Cannot
  access the Approval Queue.
- **Analyst** — merchandising and market-intelligence questions. Cannot access the Approval
  Queue.

## Why every non-Manager request goes through approval
This is broader than a dollar-amount rule. Any request submitted by a Tier 1 Support or
Analyst employee — an order-status lookup, a policy question, an analytics query, not just
a refund — is held for a Manager's sign-off before it's considered final. This is
deliberate: it gives every junior employee's output a second set of eyes before it's acted
on, the same review discipline a written report or a financial transaction would get,
rather than treating "this happens to touch money" as the only thing worth double-checking.

## How this relates to the refund threshold and anomaly check
These are two INDEPENDENT reasons a request can land in the Approval Queue, not one rule
replacing the other:
1. **The refund threshold and anomaly check** (see Returns & Refund Policy) — a refund ≥
   $250, or a pattern of rapid sub-threshold requests, requires approval regardless of who
   submitted it, including a Manager.
2. **The role-based gate** (this document) — anyone who isn't a Manager needs approval on
   EVERYTHING, regardless of dollar amount or pattern.

A Manager processing a $310 refund still needs approval (reason 1). A Tier 1 Support
employee looking up a simple order status also needs approval (reason 2), even though
nothing about that specific request — no dollar amount, no anomaly — would otherwise
trigger it.

## What this means in practice for support handling
- If you're Tier 1 Support or an Analyst, expect every request you submit to show as
  "awaiting approval" until a Manager reviews it — this is normal, not a sign something
  went wrong with your request.
- The Approval Queue shows the Manager who submitted request, which customer it's about,
  and the original message — a Manager reviewing the queue has full context without
  needing to ask the submitting employee to re-explain.
- This does not replace the Employee Escalation Procedures' guidance on when to actively
  flag something to a Manager yourself (fraud suspicion, a customer threatening legal
  action, and so on) — that's about judgment calls that go beyond what any automated gate
  catches. The role-based approval gate is a blanket review step; escalation is a
  judgment-based one. Both can apply to the same situation.
