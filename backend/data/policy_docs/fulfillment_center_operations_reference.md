# Fulfillment Center Operations Reference

## The three centers
NorthPeak ships from three fulfillment centers — US-East, US-West, and EU. Coverage areas
and standard carriers are covered in the Shipping & Delivery Policy; this document covers
what that policy doesn't: internal processing time and center-specific escalation triggers.

## Standard processing times (before the item ships)
All three centers target same-day processing for orders placed before 2pm local center
time; after that cutoff, the order processes the next business day. This is separate from
shipping transit time covered in the Shipping & Delivery Policy — a customer asking "why
hasn't my order shipped yet" on the same day they ordered is usually still within normal
processing time, not a delay.

## When to escalate to a specific center vs. general support
- **A specific item is out of stock at one center but shows available**: this is a
  center-specific inventory sync issue — escalate to that center's ops contact, don't
  promise a ship date until confirmed, since promising and then missing it creates a worse
  experience than an honest delay notice.
- **A shipment shows as delivered but the customer says it never arrived**: check which
  center and carrier handled it first — EU/DPD claims follow a different (typically slower)
  claims process than US/UPS or FedEx claims, so set expectations accordingly.
- **A batch of orders from the same center all show delays**: this usually means a
  center-wide issue (weather, system outage, inventory receiving delay), not individual
  order problems — flag to Merchandising/Ops immediately rather than handling each order
  as a one-off, per the Employee Escalation Procedures' guidance on issues affecting
  multiple customers at once.

## Cross-border orders
An order can ship from a different center than the one nearest the customer if the primary
center is out of stock — e.g. a US customer's order fulfilled from EU stock. When this
happens, transit time follows the Shipping & Delivery Policy's cross-border timeline
(7–12 business days), not the faster domestic timeline, even though the customer is in the
US. This is a common source of "why is this taking so long" tickets — checking which center
actually fulfilled the order (not just the customer's location) usually explains it.
