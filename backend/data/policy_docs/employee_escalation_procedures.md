# Employee Escalation Procedures

## When to escalate beyond the standard approval threshold
The $250 refund-approval threshold is a floor, not the only reason to escalate. Escalate to
your team lead or the on-duty manager, regardless of dollar amount, when:

- A customer explicitly threatens legal action, a chargeback, or public/social media
  complaint tied to a specific unresolved issue.
- A request involves a suspected fraud pattern — mismatched shipping/billing details, a
  new account with unusually large first-order value, or a pattern of returns that looks
  coordinated across multiple accounts.
- The same customer has contacted support 3+ times about the same unresolved issue.
- A request falls genuinely outside written policy and you're not confident which way to
  rule — escalate rather than improvise an exception.
- A vendor or fulfillment-center error affects multiple customers at once (e.g. a mislabeled
  shipment batch) — this needs Merchandising/Ops visibility, not a one-off refund.

## Who to escalate to
- **Refund/billing disputes above policy but under $1,000**: your team lead.
- **Above $1,000, or any fraud suspicion**: the on-duty manager AND Finance, same day.
- **Product safety concern** (an item that could cause injury, not just quality issues):
  escalate immediately to Merchandising leadership, regardless of order value — safety
  escalations are never held for a standard approval queue.
- **Data or security concern** (a customer reports account access issues, or you notice
  something that looks like a guardrail bypass attempt): escalate to Engineering/Security
  directly, do not attempt to resolve it yourself.

## What "escalate" means in practice
Escalating is not the same as approving or denying — it means handing the decision to
someone with the authority and context to make it, with a clear summary of what's being
asked and why it's outside your standard authority. A rushed guess to avoid escalating is a
worse outcome than a short delay while the right person weighs in.

## Documentation expectations
Every escalation should include: the customer/order/ticket reference, a one-line summary of
the issue, what you'd recommend if you had authority to decide, and why this specific case
crossed the escalation line. This is what lets a manager make a fast, informed call instead
of re-investigating from scratch.
